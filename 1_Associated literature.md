### [[Biological structure and function from learning sequences]]
This paper was the first in the ESM (Evolutionary Scale Modelling) series of [[protein language models | protein language models (pLM)]]. They show how biological structural information such as secondary structure content could be extracted from pretrained pLMs. The model was limited in mainly dataset size but they showed the importance of scaling. Bigger dataset size lead to better structural information embedded in the model. 

### [[Language Modeling Materializes a World Model of Protein Biology]]
The latest in the series of ESM [[protein language models]], describing the ESM-Cambrian. The model is not drastically bigger than earlier iterations, but is trained on much larger dataset including metagenomic sequences. They apply ESM-C embeddings for structure prediction, function annotation and residue-level interpretation of biological concepts through [[SAE | Sparse Auto Encoders (SAE)]]. 

### [[Protein language models learn evolutionary statistics of interacting sequence motifs]]
The authors of this paper wanted to understand fundamentally how [[protein language models]] could learn structural features by only training on sequences. They test whether the models simulate biophysical properties of proteins or if they memorise small fragments of patterns that can be observed from co-evolutionary statistics.
